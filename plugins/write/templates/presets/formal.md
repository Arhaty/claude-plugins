# Tone of voice

Preset: formal. For reports, policies, contracts, documentation and correspondence with
institutions. Written by `/write:setup`; edit it freely. Where this file and the `write` skill
disagree, this file wins.

## Language

Write in {{language}}. Use one dictionary and one style manual and stay consistent with them.
In English:

- Spell out numbers one through nine. Use numerals for 10 and above, and always in figures,
  measurements and money.
- Use the serial comma.
- Dates in full: "20 February 2026". Times with the zone when it matters.
- Define a term or acronym on first use and use it the same way throughout.

## Register

- **No contractions.** "Does not", "it is".
- **Third person or "we"** for the organization. "You" only in instructions addressed to the
  reader.
- **Complete sentences.** No fragments, even for emphasis.
- **Precise over plain** where the two differ: "terminate" in a contract, "end" everywhere else.
- **One term per concept.** Do not vary a defined term for style.

## Brevity

Formal is not long. Every sentence carries information, evidence or an obligation.

- Remove throat-clearing: "it should be noted that", "it is important to mention", "with regard
  to the matter of".
- "In order to" is "to". "Prior to" is "before". "In the event that" is "if".
- One idea per sentence. Long sentences become two.

## Claims and hedging

- **State what is known plainly.** Hedge only the claims that are uncertain, once each, and say
  what the uncertainty is ("the figures for Q3 are provisional").
- **Attribute every fact** to a document, a dataset or a named source.
- **No intensifiers** and no superlatives.

## Structure

- **Lead with the conclusion or the request**, then the supporting detail.
- **Numbered sections and numbered lists** where a reader will cite an item.
- **Headings name what the section holds.** Sentence case.
- **Tables** for comparisons and reference data.

## What the writing avoids

- Em dashes, and en dashes except in numeric ranges.
- Emojis, exclamation marks, rhetorical questions.
- Corporate buzzwords and marketing language.
- Passive voice that hides who is responsible. Name the actor: "the supplier will deliver", not
  "delivery will be made".
- Closing formulas that add nothing ("Should you have any questions, please do not hesitate...").
  End on the last substantive point or the action required.

## By medium

- **Letters and email.** Salutation and sign-off per the recipient's convention. Purpose in the
  first paragraph.
- **Reports.** A summary of findings at the top, method and limits stated once, findings with
  their figures.
- **Policies and contracts.** "Must" for obligations, "may" for permissions. Never "should" for an
  obligation.
