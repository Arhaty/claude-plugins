# Tone of voice

Preset: plain. Written by `/write:setup`; edit it freely. Where this file and the `write` skill
disagree, this file wins.

## Language

Write in {{language}}. Use one dictionary and one style manual and stay consistent with them.
In English:

- Spell out numbers one through nine. Use numerals for 10 and above, and always in metrics.
- Use the serial comma: it prevents ambiguity in lists.
- Percent: "5%", numeral and symbol.
- Dates: "Feb. 20, 2026" or "20 February 2026". Pick one and keep it. No "th" or "st".
- Spell out an acronym on first use, then use the acronym.

## Brevity

Every sentence earns its place. If it adds no information, evidence or forward movement, cut it.

The test: re-read each paragraph and ask what the reader learns that they didn't know from the one
before. If nothing, the paragraph is filler.

- **Word.** "In order to" is "to". "Due to the fact that" is "because". "At the present time" is
  "now".
- **Sentence.** One idea per sentence. Two commas and a semicolon is probably two sentences.
- **Paragraph.** Make the point and get out. Body paragraphs rarely need more than four sentences.
- **Document.** A short piece that says what it needs to beats a long one that says the same thing
  with padding.

## Filler

These signal that the writer is about to say something instead of saying it. Remove them.

- **Performative clarity:** "let me be clear", "to be frank", "I'll be honest", "the reality is",
  "the truth is", "make no mistake". A statement that needs a preamble announcing its honesty is
  too weak to stand on its own.
- **Throat-clearing:** "it's worth noting that", "it should be noted that", "it goes without
  saying", "needless to say", "at the end of the day", "in today's world".
- **Hedge stacking:** "I think", "I believe", "I feel like" in front of analysis; "one could argue
  that"; "perhaps", "maybe" and "might" in one sentence. One hedge per uncertain claim is honest.
  Three in one sentence is not.
- **Empty intensifiers:** "very", "really", "extremely", "incredibly", "absolutely", "literally",
  "actually". If the word can go without changing the meaning, it goes.

## Tone

**Direct, not aggressive.** State observations and evidence, draw conclusions from them, and don't
perform conviction. The aim is to say the uncomfortable thing without raising your voice.

- Do: "The audit found three unpatched servers. The fix shipped two weeks later."
- Don't: "The team recklessly left the servers wide open."

When writing criticism or disagreement:

1. **Observation over evaluation.** Describe what happened before interpreting why. Lead with
   facts, documents, data and events, and let the reader form the judgment with you.
2. **Impact over blame.** Focus on consequences and incentives, not on anyone's character. It
   makes the argument harder to dismiss.
3. **Specificity over generalization.** Name the decision, the date, the evidence and the outcome.
   Vague accusations are easy to wave away.
4. **Acknowledge complexity.** When the evidence is mixed, say so. Saying what the other side gets
   right makes the rest of the critique credible.

Strong conclusions are allowed. Language that attacks character, assigns motive without evidence,
or uses intensity in place of argument is not. The test: could the subject read it and say "the
facts are wrong" but not "this is a personal attack"?

## What the writing avoids

- **Em dashes**, and en dashes except in numeric ranges. Use commas, periods, colons or
  parentheses.
- **Corporate buzzwords:** "leverage", "synergy", "ecosystem", "learnings", "journey",
  "stakeholders".
- **Superlatives:** "incredible", "amazing", "groundbreaking", "revolutionary".
- **Engagement bait:** "Agree?", "What do you think?", "Share if you...".
- **Passive voice** where active is clearer: "the team released the report", not "the report was
  released".
- **Emojis** and **exclamation marks.**
- **Scare quotes** to signal disagreement. Use the word or challenge it directly.
- **Symmetrical structure** where every section follows the same beat, and consecutive sentences
  that all start with "This".

## Formatting

- Sentence case for headings.
- Straight quotes and apostrophes.
- No bold or italic for emphasis in body text unless the medium needs it.

## By medium

- **Web copy.** Concise. Headlines scannable. Figures exact.
- **Email.** Purpose in the first sentence. Brief and warm, no "hope this finds you well".
- **Social posts.** Short paragraphs and short sentences. Density over length.
- **Long-form articles.** Sources named and linkable. Every claim traceable.
